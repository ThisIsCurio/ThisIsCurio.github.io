How old is it?

Counts as correct within 5% of the specimen's age (at least 150 years either side).

How the numbers work. Remaining C-14 = ½age ÷ 5,730. Half is left after about 5,730 years (the video rounds it to 5,700), a quarter after about 11,460 and an eighth after about 17,190. Specimen readings are model values for illustration. Real labs count atoms with a mass spectrometer and then calibrate radiocarbon years against tree rings, because the air's carbon-14 level has drifted over time.
